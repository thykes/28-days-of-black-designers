Intro:

I grew up enamored with creating things. I've always wanted to be an artist or engineer, and now I'm a designer. So, a solid percentage of my soul is still intact. Sketching Pokemon in my youth transitioned into scribbling friends' names onto trapper keepers during lectures, then into 3D modeling and Photoshop in my young-adulthood.

Now I'm a woman with the Creative Cloud. Which, doesn't exactly make me unique, but provides me with an arsenal of tools to create, collaborate and ideate. Joking aside, I love what I do. I think one of the coolest feelings is being able to do what you love, professionally.

  type: qa
  question: Growing Up in Chicago
  answer: |
    I was born and raised as an only child in Chicago, Illinois. To give you an idea, my mom and I were the only two in my family to graduate from college, so I never really had a passion to be anything like a doctor or lawyer.
    
    I was the only girl who lived on my block, so naturally hanging with the boys became an everyday thing. All of my friends were heavily into sports, and after my mom got over the fact that I was a "girl," she allowed me to play. I ended up being the first female to play baseball for Jackie Robinson West in Chicago. I went on to play sports throughout my entire life, which is what drives my passion for primarily working in athletics now. An ACL injury in college put a halt to my career and caused me to focus more on my education.

    How did you first get interested in
    design?
  answer: |
    Growing up, my mom had to give me a pen and paper to keep me quiet in church. I still have a lot of those drawings. They're terrible, but then again I was, like, 4 or 5 years old. From then on, I filled sketchbooks with drawings up until high school.
    
    I was always a math whiz, so everyone rooted for me to be an engineer, not an artist. Still undecided, I went to college undeclared and took classes in engineering, architecture, and life drawing as an elective. I came across a study called "Industrial Design," which basically was a cross between design and engineering.

  type: qa
  question: "Tell me about the work you've done?"
  answer: |
    I've done work in a variety of fields. I actually started as a videographer shooting music videos for local artists in Chicago. That's initially how I began to build my following. A few of them went on to be big names in the industry today—I think Chance The Rapper being the biggest.  
    
    As far as design work, I started as an industrial designer in the point-of-purchase industry. If you go to Best Buy and walk around, you'll notice most products have a display that you interact with (pressing buttons, lifting, etc.). Those displays are some of the things I had the opportunity to design. After leaving industrial design, I took a job at Washington University Athletics as a Graphic Designer. One year later, I am now at Rutgers.
