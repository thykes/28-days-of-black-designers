Text:

My name is Nitashia Johnson. I graduated from Booker T. Washington High School for the Performing and Visual Arts in 2008 and became a 2012 alumna of Texas Woman's University with a BFA in Design Communication. Currently, I am a Graphic Designer at UT Dallas with an unwavering passion for art and photography. I also hold a Master of Arts in Teaching (MAT) in Art + Design Education from the Rhode Island School of Design.

In addition to my work at UT Dallas, I teach interactive design to college students at Texas Woman's University. I am also developing The Smart Project, a community-based media arts program for inner-city high school teens. This after-school program connects college mentors with high school students across North Texas to foster creative growth.

  question: >
    How did you first get interested in
    design?
  answer: |
    In junior high, an art teacher told me about Booker T. Washington High School for the Performing and Visual Arts in Dallas. She noticed my talent during my 7th-grade year and began helping me prepare for auditions.
    
    I was quite an introvert in middle school; I often stayed in her classroom during lunch and after school to create art and avoid social pressures. As we talked, I began expressing the struggles I faced at home and the hardships of growing up without parents. Moved by my story, she pushed me to refine my portfolio and urged me to research creative careers like graphic design. From that moment on, I knew I wanted to pursue design communication. I fell in love with the process, and through drive and dedication, I was admitted to the school where I began working toward my dreams.

    How can design be more accommodating to
    underrepresented populations?
  answer: "Design could be much more welcoming in its outreach to marginalized communities. There isn't enough exposure for young people early in life. The field should work toward community outreach on a larger scale. People in our communities should be introduced to design by professionals in the field, which can sometimes seem elitist from the outside looking in. I also believe the industry should focus on showcasing digital artists from a wide variety of cultural backgrounds."

    What has your experience been as a
    person of color in the design industry?
  answer: |
    I haven’t experienced many "vivid" mishaps, but I have felt the underlying tension. It’s often hard to notice roadblocks because they are indirect, making it difficult to point out specific bad intentions or judgment. At one point, I was nervous about attending interviews with my natural hair because people told me the world "wasn't ready for it." I eventually moved past those comments—they went in one ear and out the other. My personality shines bright, and I view my hair as a crown for my intellectual mind.
